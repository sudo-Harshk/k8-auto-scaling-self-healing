\begin{abstract}
Naive machine-learning Kubernetes controllers are unsafe under burst load:
the model can predict replica counts that violate resource bounds, request
conflicting actions, or trigger runaway scaling loops. We present
SHIELD-AI, a hybrid controller that combines an online machine-learning
oracle (River HoeffdingAdaptiveTreeRegressor + HalfSpaceTrees anomaly
detector) with a formally-verified safety shield expressed in TLA+. The
shield's invariants --- replica bounds, bounded scaling step, no conflicting
actions, bounded action rate, heal-does-not-scale --- are exhaustively
model-checked across $273{,}702$ distinct reachable states ($2.49$ million
state generations, depth $53$, $4$ min $6$ s on commodity hardware).
SHIELD-AI is end-to-end reproducible from a single \texttt{make demo}
target. We evaluate against HPA, KEDA, and a FIRM-style threshold
controller with $N \geq 10$ runs per scenario, paired tests (Wilcoxon),
and $95\%$ confidence intervals on every metric. Results show that
SHIELD-AI matches KEDA on latency while provably preventing the unsafe
actions observed in the ML-only ablation. We contribute: (1) a closed-form
TLA+ specification of K8s safety under bounded ML output, (2) an
online-learning controller with an actual feedback loop, and (3) a
reproducible artifact evaluated with statistical rigor.
\end{abstract}
